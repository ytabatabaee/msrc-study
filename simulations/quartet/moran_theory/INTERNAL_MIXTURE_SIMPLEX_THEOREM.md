# Internal mixture audit for the 38-state model

The proposed target formula is algebraically correct. If three distributions

\[
v_j(T)=(1-e^{-2T})e_j+e^{-2T}{\bf1}/3
\]

are mixed with weights `w`, then

\[
q=(1-e^{-2T})w+e^{-2T}{\bf1}/3.
\]

For a target `q*` with every component larger than `e^{-2T}/3`, the weights

\[
w_j=\frac{q^*_j-e^{-2T}/3}{1-e^{-2T}}
\]

are nonnegative and sum to one. The certificates in
`results/internal_mixture/target_certificates.tsv` verify this construction to
floating-point precision for vertices approached, edge, center, and interior
targets.

## Why this is not yet an MSRC model

Each `v_j(T)` in the construction is generated by a different deterministic
taxon-specific inheritance pattern: `0011`, `0101`, or `0110`. Combining them
with `w` is an external mixture over three separately parameterized MSRC
models. It is not one run of the 38-state model.

The existing regime process has only two states. Conditional on a regime at a
shared boundary, the model has two conditional genealogy laws, and the root
regime distribution supplies at most a two-component selector mixture. Regime
paths are not an additional discrete selector with three independently
adjustable masses: different paths alter the time-dependent generator and do
not conditionally reproduce the three fixed vertex constructions. Therefore
the displayed weight formula does not establish an internal three-component
mixture.

This is an obstruction to the proposed direct selector proof, not a proof that
no continuous choice of all two-regime parameters can ever map onto the whole
simplex. Full interior coverage for the unrestricted 38-state model remains
unresolved. The same is true, with stronger biological restrictions, for
identity and founding-count inheritance.

## Three-state extension

A three-regime process would have a 57-state four-lineage operator and could
provide three latent regime states. That is necessary for a direct three-state
selector construction, but it is not sufficient: each regime must generate the
corresponding topology-favoring genealogy, and the speciation kernels must be
biologically specified. Merely adding a third frequency value does not prove
simplex coverage.

The original finite-`M` Moran model does not inherit this selector argument.
Its count process and binomial daughter kernels integrate over count states and
do not provide three arbitrary topology labels.

## Status

* **Proved:** the target-weight algebra and the exact external mixture identity.
* **Proved:** the proposed three-component selector is not encoded by the two
  states of the existing regime variable.
* **Numerically verified:** external mixtures match all listed target vectors;
  the exact vertex-approaching components agree with the 38-state operator.
* **Unresolved:** whether the full continuous 38-state parameter map is
  surjective or dense in the simplex.
* **Not established:** any full-coverage theorem for biologically constrained
  inheritance or the original Moran model.
