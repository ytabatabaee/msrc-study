# Prescribed-frequency MSRC quartet recursion

This result conditions on a supplied history of background frequencies. On
each branch (b), the history is a young-to-old sequence

\[
\mathcal P_b=((p_{b,1},\Delta t_{b,1}),\ldots,(p_{b,J_b},\Delta t_{b,J_b})).
\]

The frequencies are treated as fixed while the genealogy is propagated. The
calculation therefore has 2, 6, 11, and 19 genealogy states for one through
four extant lineages, independently of the Moran copy number (M). It is
exact conditional on these histories; it is not the unconditional finite-(M)
Moran calculation.

## Conditional generators and time ordering

For a frequency (p), the background switches are

\[
0\to1: \rho p,\qquad 1\to0: \rho(1-p),
\]

and same-background pair coalescence uses the simulator convention

\[
c_0(p)=\{M\max(1-p,1/M)\}^{-1},\qquad
c_1(p)=\{M\max(p,1/M)\}^{-1}.
\]

The generators (G_n(p)) include transient background configurations and,
for (n\ge2), absorbing topology states. For a row distribution (v),

\[
v_{\rm old}=v_{\rm young}\prod_{j=1}^{J_b}
\exp\{G_n(p_{b,j})\Delta t_{b,j}\},
\]

where the product is applied in increasing age order. Generators at different
frequencies need not commute, so the order is part of the definition.
At (p=0) or (1), impossible terminal samples have zero mass; the finite-copy
floor in the coalescence rate follows the existing simulator convention.

## Balanced tree

For `((1,2),(3,4))`, the two terminal pairs are propagated through their
sister branches. Let (s_L) and (s_R) be the surviving unresolved masses.
The early contribution is

\[
q_{\rm early}=(1-s_Ls_R,0,0).
\]

The unresolved background vector (F_{\rm bal}\in\mathbb R^{19}) is the
product of the two pair distributions conditional on their backgrounds. The
root contribution is

\[
q_{\rm root,i}=F_{\rm bal}^{\mathsf T}P_4(\mathcal P_A)h_i,
\qquad
q_i=q_{\rm early,i}+q_{\rm root,i}.
\]

Here (P_4(\mathcal P_A)) is the ordered product of four-lineage matrix
exponentials and (h_i) is the existing residual-topology vector.

## Pectinate tree

For `(((1,2),3),4)`, the sister pair is embedded in the 11-state
three-lineage generator on the intermediate branch. Its three absorbing states
are (Q_1,Q_2,Q_3). If (c_i) is the mass absorbed into topology (i) before
the root and (F_{\rm pect}\in\mathbb R^{19}) is the surviving four-lineage
entry vector, then

\[
q_i=c_i+F_{\rm pect}^{\mathsf T}P_4(\mathcal P_A)h_i.
\]

The construction keeps the absorbing mass separate from the root-entry mass,
so early events are counted exactly once.

## Terminal-pattern joint law

With `with_joint=True`, the implementation also returns

\[
J[z,i]=P(Q_i,Z=z\mid\mathcal P),
\]

without normalizing the pattern-specific vectors. It reports

\[
P(Z=z)=\sum_iJ[z,i],\quad
P(Q_i\mid\mathcal P)=\sum_zJ[z,i],\quad
P(Q_i\mid Z=z,\mathcal P)=J[z,i]/P(Z=z).
\]

Zero-probability patterns have undefined conditional probabilities (`NaN`).
These identities are checked in the test suite.

## Relation to the finite-(M) Moran result

If \(\mathcal P\) is generated by the Moran process, the unconditional law is

\[
P(Q_i)=\mathbb E_{\mathcal P}\left[
q_{\rm early,i}(\mathcal P)+
F(\mathcal P)^{\mathsf T}P_4(\mathcal P)h_i
\right].
\]

The expectation must be taken after evaluating each jointly generated history.
Replacing the history by its mean frequency, averaging generators, or using a
single prescribed trajectory is a different model. The existing finite-(M)
count-indexed solver remains the reference for the exact unconditional
hierarchical model.

The current implementation conditions on branch histories that already encode
the required boundary frequencies. It consequently does not apply a second
binomial daughter-count inheritance kernel. To reproduce the simulator's
hierarchical model by trajectory averaging, histories must be generated jointly
with the same speciation and origin conventions.
