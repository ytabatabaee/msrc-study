# Autonomous research on conditional Moran simplex coverage

## Resolution

**FULL COVERAGE UNRESOLVED.** The original finite-\( M \) Moran MSRC
model has a rigorously identifiable one-dimensional coverage structure, but no
global theorem or global obstruction has been found.

## New exact result: radial segment theorem

Fix either rooted quartet shape. Use equal branch copy counts, an interior
point-mass initial Moran count, neutral dynamics, zero background switching,
zero terminal/sister/intermediate durations, and vary only the root duration
\( T\ge0 \). For each paired pattern

\[
0011\to Q_1,\qquad 0101\to Q_2,\qquad 0110\to Q_3,
\]

the exact conditional law lies on the corresponding median radial line:

\[
q_j(T)=d(T),\qquad
q_k(T)=q_\ell(T)=\frac{1-d(T)}2.
\]

At \( T=0 \), the root contributes the unresolved quartet law and

\[
q(0)=(1/3,1/3,1/3).
\]

As \( T\to\infty \), the compatible pair coalesces with probability
tending to one, so \( d(T)\to1 \). The conditional law is continuous in
\( T \), and the conditioning event has positive probability for every
finite \( T \). Therefore the intermediate-value theorem implies that
every point on the open radial segment from the simplex center to the selected
vertex is attained at some finite duration (or, at minimum, in the closure if
one allows an endpoint).

### Why the symmetry holds

In this subfamily, the paired arrangement pattern creates two compatible
background matches related by the neutral background-complement and taxon
exchange symmetries of the root recursion. The two discordant resolutions have
identical absorption probabilities. The joint pattern recursion preserves the
same symmetry before conditional normalization, so it applies to `q`, not
only to an unconditional average.

This is a genuine single Moran parameter family. It does not average separate
parameter settings.

## Why this does not settle the simplex

The radial theorem controls the dominant probability and forces the
transverse coordinate

\[
r=q_1/(q_1+q_3)
\]

to equal one-half in the `0101` family. The previous exact pectinate
certificates show that lower-branch asymmetries can move `r` to either side of
one-half, but they do not prove that `r` spans the full admissible interval at
fixed `d`.

The missing global statement is therefore sharper than “find a nonzero
Jacobian”: for every \( d \) in the `Q2` sector, the level set

\[
\{\theta:q_2(0101;\theta)=d\}
\]

must have an `r`-image containing the whole sector interval, or its closure.
No monotonicity, connectedness, endpoint, or degree result for these exact
finite-M matrix-exponential level sets is known. The analogous statements for
the other two patterns are equally open.

## Approaches evaluated

* **Vertex limits:** establish only endpoint behavior; they do not supply
  convexity or an internal mixture.
* **Local Jacobians:** establish local two-dimensional sensitivity when
  numerically nonzero, but no global image or boundary mapping.
* **Asymmetry perturbations:** disprove a simple `q1 >= q3` obstruction, but
  do not hold `d` fixed.
* **External convex mixtures:** reproduce arbitrary simplex points only by
  randomizing between separately parameterized Moran models, which violates
  the single-model requirement.
* **Root-stage race decomposition:** gives a useful positive-kernel
  representation, but the lower-branch Moran recursion does not currently
  provide independently controllable weights for all three topology outcomes.

## Consequences

The exact result proves coverage of three radial one-dimensional families,
not the two-dimensional simplex. Full conditional coverage, density, and a
quartet-frequency introgression indistinguishability theorem remain
unresolved. Equality of quartet frequencies, if eventually established, would
also concern only that conditional observable and not the full genealogy or
genome-wide distribution.
