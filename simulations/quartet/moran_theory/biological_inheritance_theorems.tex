\paragraph{Inheritance-restricted conclusions.}
For the two-regime model, let $H$ be a row-stochastic parent-to-daughter
regime kernel and let $q(H)$ denote the exact unconditional quartet law.

\begin{proposition}[Equal-regime reduction]
If $p_L=p_H$, then $q(H)$ is independent of the regime transition matrices,
the initial regime distribution, and the inheritance kernels, and equals the
fixed-frequency genealogy at the common frequency.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two genealogy blocks of every joint regime operator are identical. The
regime chain is conservative and can be marginalized from the Kronecker-sum
semigroup. The same factorization applies at every inheritance event.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Symmetry condition]
If a taxon permutation exchanges $Q_2$ and $Q_3$ and leaves the complete
parameterized tree, branch durations, population parameters, terminal
sampling, regime process, and inheritance kernels invariant, then $q_2=q_3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The permutation is a measure-preserving bijection on regime and genealogy
paths and exchanges the two absorbing topology labels.
\end{proof}

The search reported with this note found no unconditional anomaly for the
identity, symmetric, or predeclared founding kernels on its finite grid. This
is a numerical result, not a proof of $q_1\geq\max(q_2,q_3)$ under identity
inheritance. An unrestricted-kernel numerical witness and an exact match to a
two-component network MSC mixture are also reported, but neither implies a
positive-area overlap or full-simplex coverage.
