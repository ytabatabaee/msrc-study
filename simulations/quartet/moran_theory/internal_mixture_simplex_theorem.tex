\paragraph{Target mixtures and the two-state obstruction.}
Let
\[
v_j(T)=(1-e^{-2T})e_j+e^{-2T}{\bf1}/3.
\]
For any $q^*$ satisfying $q^*_j>e^{-2T}/3$, define
\[
w_j=\frac{q^*_j-e^{-2T}/3}{1-e^{-2T}}.
\]
Then $w_j\geq0$, $\sum_jw_j=1$, and
$\sum_jw_jv_j(T)=q^*$. This is an exact identity for an external mixture of
three separately parameterized models.

It is not an internal mixture theorem for the 38-state model. That model has
only two regime states at a shared boundary. Conditioning on that state gives
two conditional genealogy laws, so a direct latent-selector construction has at
most two independently weighted components. Regime histories change the
generator through time and do not supply three fixed topology-specific
components. A third regime would be a different 57-state model and would still
require a separate proof that its regimes generate the desired components.

Thus the weight identity proves neither exact interior coverage nor density for
the existing two-regime model.
