# Results

Structural exposure increased with descendant-clade size. Existing internal
origins exposed 22.5%, 34.5%, 83.7%, and 100% of quartets in the four pilot
trees, depending on the number of descendants. The deep internal scenario
exposed 34.5%, 93.1%, 95.2%, and 100%, while root scenarios exposed all
27,405 quartets by the structural criterion.

Forward persistence did not increase monotonically with origin age. The audit
contains one history per tree/scenario, so these values are diagnostic rather
than inferential. In several trees, long exposure produced fixation or loss,
showing why older origin alone cannot be assumed to increase informative
polymorphism. Averaged over the four trees, the observed discordant 2:2
terminal-pattern frequency was about 0.0014 for A, 0.0390 for B, 0.0020 for C,
and 0 for D in this single-history audit. These are terminal arrangement
frequencies, not quartet-topology reversal probabilities.

No valid genealogy-level quartet margins, reversal frequencies, or ASTRAL
results were produced. The local backward run was dominated by 30-taxon
ancestral coalescence and was stopped. Accordingly, the experiment does not
answer whether deeper origins cause population-level quartet reversal.
