# Results

The three effective switching intensities generate clearly different realized
switching histories.  Across the retained pilot, mean switches per genealogy
were approximately 31.6 at rho=0.02, 14.9 at rho=0.01, and 3.73 at
rho=0.002.  The mean zero-switch fractions were 0.040, 0.049, and 0.070,
respectively.  Thus the absence of an ASTRAL trend is not caused by the three
treatments having indistinguishable switching behavior.

The origin is on an internal branch in every pilot replicate, so only a
descendant clade carries the structured process.  At the younger end of the
007 origin branch N8, empirical polymorphism probabilities were about 0.31,
0.28, and 0.30 for rho=0.02, 0.01, and 0.002.  Persistence varies strongly
among descendant branches and replicates; it is not summarized by a single
global frequency.  After classifying each pattern against the induced
species-tree split (rather than the arbitrary order of the quartet tuple), the
mean discordant 2:2 terminal-pattern probability over the diagnostic panel was
approximately 0.057, 0.086, and 0.080 for the three treatments.  The earlier
position-based values were invalid for this mixed taxon panel.  These are
natural pattern frequencies, not conditional sampling probabilities.

The pilot therefore supports a mixed explanation: switching changes
substantially, but the structured process is spatially/cladewise restricted,
polymorphism is heterogeneous across descendant branches, and arrangement
patterns are diluted by uniform and 3:1 configurations.  A 30-taxon species
tree estimator can remain unchanged when affected quartet shifts are spread
across a minority of the full quartet set.  The present retrospective audit
does not establish whether conditional topology effects are large enough to
reverse a quartet distribution in a biologically plausible expanded-rate
regime.
