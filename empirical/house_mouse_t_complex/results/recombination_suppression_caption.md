# Figure caption

**Independent recombination suppression and heterogeneous local quartet support across the house-mouse t-complex.** (A) Four historical t-complex inversions are shown as colored intervals on the GRCm38/mm10 chromosome 17 coordinate system: In(17)1, 5,747,331–6,957,919 bp; In(17)2, 7,435,308–13,016,510 bp; In(17)3, 14,068,229–18,051,507 bp; and In(17)4, 19,401,000–40,049,964 bp. Coordinates are the wild-type reference intervals from Swanepoel et al. (2026), with provenance in t_complex_inversion_provenance.md. The gray coverage band marks the approximate 5-kb local-gene-tree interval, chr17:5.00–37.05 Mb; In(17)4 extends beyond the available tree windows. T and tf are approximate modern marker anchors at 8.43 and 27.3 Mb, respectively, and the bracket identifies the historical T–tf marker interval. (B) Independent historical linkage evidence for recombination suppression between T and tf. Horizontal bars show the published genetic-map ranges of 7–12 cM for the conventional map and 0.1–0.5 cM for naturally occurring t/+ heterozygotes from Silver & Artzt (1981). The bars represent reported ranges rather than confidence intervals or precise point estimates. Hammer, Bliss & Silver (1991) additionally summarized approximately 100–200-fold suppression across the broader t-complex; that region-wide estimate is reported in the text and is not plotted as a continuous map or assigned to individual inversions. Genetic distance in cM is not converted to cM/Mb. (C) Frozen local quartet support from the Kelemen & Vicoso-derived all-tip scan. Faint points are the original 5-kb windows and colored lines are documented 250-kb means for q_species, q_t-alt, and q_other. The lower trace shows D(w)=q_t-alt(w)−q_species(w): positive values indicate greater support for the t-associated alternative quartet, whereas negative values indicate greater support for the species quartet. Gray gaps indicate missing retained windows and are not interpolated. The structural and linkage evidence is independent of these local genealogies; historical crosses and local gene trees come from different samples and experimental designs, so spatial concordance should not be interpreted causally.
