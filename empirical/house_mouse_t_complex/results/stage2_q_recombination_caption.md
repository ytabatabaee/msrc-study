# Quartet/recombination-track caption

The narrow panel above panel A shows the four historical t-complex inversion
boundaries using the GRCm38/mm10 wild-type coordinates in Swanepoel et al.
(2026) Supplementary Table 1. The four historical intervals correspond to
Swanepoel Inv1, Inv3, Inv4, and Inv5; the study also reports a novel overlapping
Inv2, which is documented separately and is not forced into the historical
four-track display. The coordinates are sequence-based and their junctions
were PCR/Sanger validated. The archived windows use the same mm10/GRCm38
coordinate system. Windows intersecting an inversion boundary are excluded
from the supplementary inside/outside summary.

Direct local quartet support across the published chr17 5-kb windows is shown
for all available tips, with lightly plotted raw windows and fixed 250-kb
means. `q_species`, `q_t_alt`, and `q_other` identify biological quartet
topologies and are not globally equivalent to ASTRAL q1/q2/q3. The lower
tracks directly use the authors' archived per-window topology classifications,
mapped to their source-defined phylogeny-based recombination-state categories
for domesticus, musculus, and castaneus in nonoverlapping 500-kb bins. The
y-axis is the fraction of 5-kb windows in each 500-kb bin assigned to each
source-defined class. This is not a direct recombination-rate estimate in
cM/Mb. The aligned display is descriptive and does not treat adjacent windows
as independent observations. Individual 5-kb windows can be highly decisive,
including |q_species - q_t_alt| near one, while nearby windows often favor
different genealogies. Consequently, 250-kb means frequently lie near one
third. The House-mouse data therefore do not show a long spatially coherent
near-fixed alternative-topology block. Light gray intervals labeled “No
retained local-tree windows” indicate missing retained windows and do not mean
zero support or no recombination.
