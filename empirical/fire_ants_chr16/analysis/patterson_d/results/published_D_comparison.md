# Comparison with published fire-ant D statistics

## Helleu et al. (2022), PNAS

Helleu et al., “Radiation and hybridization underpin the spread of the fire ant social supergene,” analyzed 185 haploid males: 107 SB, 58 Sb, and 20 outgroup samples from *S. saevissima* and *S. metallica*. Their calls were mapped to the *S. invicta* SB reference GCA_009650705.1 with bwa-mem and FreeBayes `-ploidy 1`, and filtered to remove indels, low quality (QUAL < 30), and sites missing in more than half of individuals. Their SI Tables S2 and S5 report inversion-specific and chromosome 1–15 D tests. Inversion tests compared one focal Sb individual with its conspecific SB and a heterospecific SB, plus one external outgroup, using AdmixTools/admixr; sample quartets were randomly drawn for 5,000 iterations per inversion. Their genome-wide analysis used species triplets and two outgroup species; significance used a 100-SNP block jackknife and Benjamini–Yekutieli correction.

The PNAS results found strong excess sharing for specific comparisons, including chromosome 1–15 D=0.18 between *S. invicta*/AdRX and *S. macdonaghi* and D=0.03 for *S. invicta*/AdRX with *S. megergates* (both reported P<0.001). Their SI inversion tests support a sequential inversion history: In(16)3 oldest, In(16)1 next, In(16)2 most recent. They infer the complete Sb supergene formed in *S. richteri*, then moved first into *S. invicta*/AdRX and subsequently into other species.

## Stolle et al. (2022), Nature Communications

The present analysis uses the Stolle et al. deposited VCF and sample mapping, associated with a different reference (`gng20170922wFex.fa` / Si_gnGA) and a release of 368 GT:DP samples. The local tree analysis uses 213 nonuniform windows, each built from four single-copy BUSCO genes, and has 267 tree-tip samples. Our D quartet groups are the Stage-1 frozen population/arrangement groups, with Argentina-restricted primary membership and *S. saevissima* as outgroup. They are not the PNAS species-level random quartet tests and do not reproduce the PNAS inversion boundaries or its complete genome alignments.

Stolle et al. interpret their coalescent trees, topology weighting, and shared-allele analyses as recurrent adaptive introgression, including an origin of Sb in the *S. invicta*/macdonaghi lineage and transfers among species. Helleu et al. instead infer assembly of the complete three-inversion supergene in *S. richteri* followed by transfer into *S. invicta*/AdRX and other lineages. These are materially different proposed source directions and evolutionary histories. They used different samples, references, call sets, windows, and hypotheses; their estimates are external biological context, not direct replication of this local four-population analysis.

The current D results are compatible with an excess of shared derived variation among the frozen Sb-bearing population contrasts in the supergene. They do not identify a donor, recipient, event count, timing, or direction, and they cannot adjudicate the competing Stolle and Helleu histories alone. Patterson's D is not uniquely diagnostic of introgression under ancestral population structure, selection, gene conversion, or mapping bias.
