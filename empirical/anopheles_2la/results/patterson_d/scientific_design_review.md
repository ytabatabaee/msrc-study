# Scientific design review

The frozen designs are retained exactly as A_merus, B_merus, C_merus,
D_merus, and the corresponding melas sensitivity tests. A and B have the
larger ingroup groups and use A. merus as the primary outgroup. C and D have a
smaller inverted coluzzii group and are sensitivity contrasts. Melas tests
change the outgroup and therefore test polarization sensitivity.

The sister-species baseline for gambiae and coluzzii, with merus branching
earlier in the complex, makes `((P1,P2),P3),O` a useful conditional population
baseline. It does not make arrangement-defined P1/P2 groups ordinary species
populations. Consequently, these are best described as exploratory
arrangement-associated ABBA–BABA site-pattern contrasts. A conventional
introgression interpretation would require stronger independence between the
population-tree hypothesis and the inversion-associated signal.

Inside 2La, ancestral arrangement polymorphism, linked genealogical history,
geographic structure, incomplete lineage sorting, and gene flow can all violate
the simple rooted null. The pilot therefore establishes a strong change in
nucleotide site-pattern asymmetry across the frozen windows, not its cause.
The merus and melas comparisons are sensitivity results; neither was selected
post hoc for signal magnitude. A. christyi was not present in the authenticated
sample set and was not downloaded.

The primary and MAC>=2 pilot D values are identical for all 64 window/design
pairs, while MAC>=2 reduces the number of passing sites. This is an observed
property of these calls: the removed low-MAC sites contribute zero ABBA/BABA
under the estimator. It is not a general mathematical identity, so both
filters remain in the full-scan output.
