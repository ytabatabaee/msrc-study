# Quartet design validation

The ordered rooted population design is `((P1,P2),P3),O`. A = (G_s,G_i,C_s,O); B = (G_s,G_i,C_i,O); C = (C_s,C_i,G_s,O); D = (C_s,C_i,G_i,O), where G is A. gambiae, C is A. coluzzii, and s/i are frozen standard/inverted classes. Frozen counts are A: 8,12,8,9 (merus) or O=4 (melas); B: 8,12,3,9/4; C: 8,3,8,9/4; D: 8,3,12,9/4. The full sample IDs are preserved in `results/patterson_d/frozen_quartet_designs.tsv`; per-sample source country/locality, karyotype, and sequence-availability flags are in `data/anopheles_2la/metadata/sample_manifest.tsv`.

Three claims must remain distinct. First, the broader species-tree baseline places gambiae and coluzzii as sisters and merus as an early branch. Second, the tested quartet is an arrangement-defined population grouping, not a pure species-tree quartet. Third, an expectation E[D]=0 needs exchangeability of P1/P2 lineages under the null, correct polarization, and a sampling model without systematic population structure/ancestry differences that favor one pairing. The plausible species tree alone does not supply that exchangeability.

A/B share the same G_s/G_i samples but change C_s versus C_i as P3. C/D reuse those same samples with reversed species roles. They are sensitivity contrasts, not independent replications. C_i has only 3 samples. Outgroup swap from merus to melas tests polarization/sample sensitivity but does not remove ingroup geographic confounding. The sample metadata show population/country/locality as valid fields, but the frozen design does not balance geography within arrangement. No defensible geography-adjusted contrast can be formed from pooled outputs.
